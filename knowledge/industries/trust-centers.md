# Trust Centers — Industry Knowledge

## Sector Overview
Trust Centers, also known as Trust Service Providers (TSPs),
Certification Authorities (CAs), and PKI Service Bureaus,
are organizations whose core business is operating the
cryptographic trust infrastructure that underpins digital
transactions, electronic signatures, digital identities,
and secure communications. They are the custodians of
digital trust — their CA certificates are embedded in
operating systems, browsers, and applications worldwide,
making them the root of trust for billions of online
interactions every day.

Trust Centers range from national government authorities —
such as the Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)
in India, CCA Bangladesh, and equivalent bodies in
Malaysia, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, and across
Africa and Asia — to commercial CAs like DigiCert,
GlobalSign, and Entrust, to enterprise-operated trust
service providers serving a specific sector or region.
What they share is the requirement to operate their
PKI infrastructure to the highest standards of security,
audit compliance, and operational reliability — because
their trustworthiness is the foundation on which all
relying parties depend.

Nexus has deep experience serving national PKI authorities
and commercial trust service providers across Asia, the
Middle East, Africa, and Europe. We understand that Trust
Centers are not just PKI customers — they are PKI operators
who need a platform they can build a commercial or

with the audits and standards their regulators require,
and operationally resilient enough to serve as national
digital infrastructure.

## Regional Trust Center Landscape

### South Asia

**India — Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA)**
The CCA is the apex body for PKI in India under the
Information Technology Act 2000. The CCA operates the
Root Certifying Authority of India (RCAI) and licenses
commercial CAs under it. Licensed CAs in India include
eMudhra, CDAC, NSDL, Sify, and others. India has one
of the most active Trust Center markets in Asia driven
by:
- MCA21 — Ministry of Corporate Affairs mandatory
  digital signatures for company filing
- Income Tax Department — digital signatures for
  IT returns
- GST — digital signatures for tax invoices
- DGFT — digital signatures for export documentation
- eSigning using Aadhaar-based eKYC and OTP

Nexus positioning: CA platform for licensed CAs under
CCA India seeking modern PKI infrastructure replacing
aging legacy platforms. Compliance with CCA guidelines
on CA operations, HSM requirements, and audit standards.

**Bangladesh — Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA Bangladesh)**
Bangladesh established its PKI framework under the
ICT Act 2006. The CCA Bangladesh licenses commercial
CAs for digital signature services supporting:
- National Board of Revenue e-filing
- Bangladesh Bank — financial transaction signatures
- Trade facilitation — export and import documentation
- Company registration digital signatures
- Government procurement portal signatures

Nexus positioning: CA platform for licensed CAs under
CCA Bangladesh. Understanding of Bangladesh ICT Act
requirements, HSM mandates, and audit requirements for
licensed CAs.

**Pakistan — National Telecommunication and Information Technology Security Board (NTISB)**
Pakistan's PKI framework supports:
- SECP — Securities and Exchange Commission filing
- FBR — Federal Board of Revenue digital signatures
- NADRA integration for identity verification

**Sri Lanka — ICTA Sri Lanka**
Growing PKI program supporting government digital
services and commercial digital signatures.

### Southeast Asia

**Malaysia — Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC)**
Malaysia has one of the most mature PKI ecosystems

MSC Trustgate, DigiCert Malaysia, and Mimos are
licensed CAs serving:
- MyCC — Malaysia Competition Commission filings
- Customs department digital signatures
- Company secretarial digital signatures
- Legal document signing

**Singapore — Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)**
Singapore's trusted framework supports:
- CorpPass — corporate digital identity
- SingPass — national identity integration
- Commercial CA licensing for document signing

**Indonesia — BSSN (National Cyber and Crypto Agency)**
Rapidly growing PKI market for:
- Directorate General of Taxes e-filing
- Ministry of Trade digital signatures
- BPOM pharmaceutical regulatory submissions

**Philippines — Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT)**
- PhilSys national ID PKI integration
- BIR digital signatures for tax compliance
- PSA civil registry digital services

### Middle East and Africa

**UAE — Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA)**
UAE has advanced Trust Center infrastructure:

- ADG — Abu Dhabi Government digital services

**Saudi Arabia — National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA)**
- Qualified Trust Service Providers under ZATCA
- VAT invoice digital signatures — massive volume
- Etimad government platform digital signatures
- National identity integration

**Egypt — Information Technology Industry Development Agency (ITIDA)**
- Egyptian NTRA digital signature licensing
- Tax authority digital signatures
- Commercial CA ecosystem development

**Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana — Emerging Markets**
Growing PKI programs driven by:
- Revenue authority digital signature requirements
- Company registry digital filings
- Financial sector digital identity
- Mobile money identity infrastructure

## Key Drivers and Challenges

### Drivers
- Government mandates for digital signatures on
  tax, company, and regulatory filings creating
  immediate commercial demand for licensed CA services
- National digital transformation programs requiring
  PKI infrastructure as foundational component
- Economic growth in South and Southeast Asia
  creating large addressable markets for digital
  identity and signature services
- Cross-border recognition agreements (ASEAN,
  GCC, EU) driving need for internationally
  compliant trust service infrastructure
- Financial inclusion initiatives requiring PKI
  for digital KYC and account opening at scale
- eGovernment service delivery creating demand
  for citizen authentication and signing services
- Private sector digital transformation requiring
  legally valid electronic contracts

### Challenges
- Regulatory complexity — Trust Centers must comply
  with national CA policy frameworks, audit standards,
  and HSM requirements that vary by country
- WebTrust and ETSI audit requirements — high cost
  and operational burden of maintaining accreditations
  that browser programs require
- Root store inclusion — commercial CAs must be
  included in Microsoft, Apple, Google, Mozilla
  root stores — a complex and ongoing compliance
  program
- Competition from global commercial CAs — DigiCert,
  GlobalSign, Sectigo competing in local markets
  with established infrastructure
- Technology refresh cycles — CA platforms deployed
  10+ years ago need modernization to support new
  protocols and certificate types
- Algorithm migration — SHA-1 to SHA-256 and future
  post-quantum migration creates operational
  challenges for trust centers
- Key ceremony complexity — generating and protecting
  root CA keys with appropriate ceremony and audit
  is operationally demanding

## Regulatory and Compliance Requirements

### International Audit Standards
- WebTrust for Certification Authorities — AICPA/CPA
  Canada standard required for browser root store
  inclusion. Covers: CA operations, PKI controls,
  certificate issuance and management.
- WebTrust for SSL Baseline Requirements
- WebTrust for Extended Validation
- ETSI EN 319 401 — General Policy Requirements
  for Trust Service Providers
- ETSI EN 319 411-1 — Policy and security requirements
  for CAs issuing publicly trusted certificates
- ETSI EN 319 411-2 — Requirements for CAs issuing
  EU qualified certificates
- ETSI EN 319 421 — Policy and security requirements
  for TSPs providing time-stamping services

### CA/Browser Forum Requirements
- Baseline Requirements for the Issuance and
  Management of Publicly-Trusted TLS Certificates
- Extended Validation Guidelines
- Baseline Requirements for Code Signing Certificates
- S/MIME Baseline Requirements

### National CA Policy Frameworks
- India: CCA Guidelines for Certifying Authorities
  under IT Act 2000 — HSM requirements, audit
  requirements, certificate profile specifications
- Bangladesh: CCA Bangladesh technical standards
  for licensed CAs
- Malaysia: MSC CA Act and MCMC guidelines
- UAE: TDRA Trust Service Provider requirements
- Saudi Arabia: NCA Trust Service Provider policy

### Cryptographic Requirements
- FIPS 140-2 Level 3 or higher for HSM protecting
  CA private keys — typically mandatory in national
  CA policy frameworks
- Key ceremony requirements for Root CA key generation
- Algorithm requirements specified by national
  cryptographic policy

## Typical PKI and IAM Requirements

### CA Platform Requirements
- Multi-tier CA hierarchy: Root CA (offline) →
  Subordinate CA → Issuing CA
- High-volume certificate issuance capability:
  Millions of certificates annually for large TSPs
- Certificate profile management for multiple
  certificate types: TLS, email, code signing,
  document signing, qualified
- OCSP responder with 99.999% availability SLA
- CRL publication to multiple repositories
- Automated certificate lifecycle management
- Certificate transparency log submission
- HSM integration for all CA private keys
- Key ceremony tooling and documentation
- Subscriber management and enrollment portal
- Audit logging of all certificate operations

### Compliance and Audit Infrastructure
- Audit log integrity — tamper-evident audit logs
  for WebTrust and ETSI audit evidence
- Change management controls for CA system changes
- Incident response procedures for mis-issuance
- Certificate revocation capability within 24 hours
  of mis-issuance detection (CA/B Forum requirement)
- Annual WebTrust or ETSI audit

### Operational Requirements
- Certificate issuance SLA: Typically seconds for
  DV, minutes for OV, hours for EV
- OCSP response time: Sub-100ms
- CA system availability: 99.999% for OCSP and CRL
- Disaster recovery: RTO typically < 4 hours
- Geographic redundancy: Active-active in some
  national CA policies

## Common Use Cases

### 1. National CA Authority Infrastructure
Government body such as CCA India or CCA Bangladesh
deploys Nexus as the CA platform for their national
PKI. Root CA operated in offline HSM vault with
formal key ceremony. Subordinate and Issuing CAs
for licensed CA policy enforcement. Nexus provides
the CA software platform while the national authority
operates the policy framework and licenses commercial
CAs beneath their trust hierarchy.

### 2. Licensed Commercial CA Platform
Commercial CA licensed by CCA India or equivalent
national authority deploys Nexus NCM as their
CA platform. Issues digital signature certificates
to enterprise and individual subscribers for
income tax, GST, MCA filings, and commercial
document signing. High-volume issuance — large
Indian CAs issue millions of certificates annually.
OCSP deployed for real-time revocation. Integration
with Aadhaar-based eKYC for subscriber identity
verification.

### 3. Regional Qualified TSP
Trust Center seeking eIDAS qualified status or
equivalent regional qualified trust service
recognition deploys Nexus platform meeting ETSI
EN 319 411-2 requirements. Qualified certificate
issuance for legal electronic signatures. Qualified
timestamp authority. Annual ETSI audit supported
by Nexus audit logging and evidence collection
capability.

### 4. Trust Center Technology Modernization
Established Trust Center operating a legacy CA
platform from 10+ years ago migrates to Nexus
NCM for technology refresh. Migration preserves
existing CA hierarchy and issued certificates.
New platform supports modern protocols —
ACME for automation, REST API for integration,
Certificate Transparency log submission. Improved
operational tooling and audit compliance capability.

### 5. Enterprise Trust Center
Large enterprise — bank, government ministry,
or multinational corporation — establishes an
internal Trust Center using Nexus to issue
certificates for internal use without depending
on commercial CAs. Corporate root CA trusted
within the enterprise. Automated TLS certificate
issuance for internal systems. Employee and
device certificate management.
